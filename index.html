02Finding work you can actually be hired for

If you need a role that hires across borders without a work permit, the useful trick is to search the payment mechanism — companies put it in the job title. Pick a preset, then adjust the location above.

Read the location line on every result. Contractor-via-platform postings are often scoped to one region — US companies commonly use these arrangements for Latin America nearshore, European companies for Eastern Europe and the Caucasus. A posting saying "must be authorized to work in X" or "W2 only, no C2C" is a hard no regardless of how remote it looks.

04Skip the boards — search company ATS pages directly

Every job exists on a Lever / Greenhouse / Ashby / Workable / Teamtailor page before any aggregator indexes it. Searching those domains finds roles earlier, free, with no board subscription. Often the highest-yield channel there is.

05Other boards worth a pass

Free to browse and apply. Uses the keywords from box 01 where the board supports it.

You never need a paid job-board account. Aggregators are indexes — each one links to a posting that lives on the company's own site, where applying is free. If a board paywalls its results, use the free tier to harvest company names, then apply direct.

06Three things worth knowing before you trust a filter

1 · "Remote" means remote within that country. A worldwide + remote + contract search returns results reading "Mexico (Remote)", "India (Remote)", "United States (Remote)". Each still expects local work authorization. Remote is not the same as globally hireable, and no filter expresses the difference — you have to read the posting.
2 · The Contract job-type filter deletes almost everything real. Measured: filtering Contract against one country returned 1 result, versus 83 unfiltered. Companies hiring international contractors still tag the posting Full-time — the arrangement lives in the contract, not in that field. Leave job type on Any and put the arrangement in your keywords instead.
3 · Aggregator "Anywhere in the World" tags are unreliable. On one major remote board, listings tagged "Anywhere in the World" said "Remote US" in the posting body. Treat every region tag as a hint and verify on the source page.
